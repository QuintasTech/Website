Every enterprise is under pressure to show results from AI. Many pilots look impressive in demonstrations and then struggle in production. The most common reason is not the model. It is the data: incomplete, inconsistent, poorly documented, scattered across systems and owned by nobody in particular.

Whether you are building predictive models or connecting generative AI assistants to company information, the same foundations apply.

## Why data matters more than the model

Modern AI models are widely available. Your data is what makes AI useful for your business, and also what makes it fail:

- A forecasting model trained on order data with duplicate customers and inconsistent product codes will produce unreliable forecasts.
- An AI assistant connected to a document library full of outdated policies will confidently give outdated answers.
- An assistant connected to files with loose permissions may surface information to people who should not see it.

## Five foundations

### 1. Know what data you have

Build a catalog of key data sets: where they live, what they contain, who owns them and how current they are. Tools such as Microsoft Purview and other data catalogs can automate much of the discovery.

### 2. Assign ownership

Every important data domain (customers, products, suppliers, employees, assets) should have a business owner accountable for its quality and definitions. IT can provide the tools; the business must own the meaning.

### 3. Measure and improve quality

Define quality rules for critical data: completeness, accuracy, consistency, timeliness and uniqueness. Measure them, publish the results and fix problems at the source rather than in each downstream report.

### 4. Get permissions right

Before connecting AI assistants to SharePoint, file shares or other repositories, review access permissions. Oversharing that went unnoticed for years becomes visible immediately when an assistant can search everything a user can technically access. Apply sensitivity labels to confidential content.

### 5. Retire what is stale

Archive or delete outdated documents and redundant data sets. Less clutter means better answers and lower storage and security risk.

## Start with one use case

Do not attempt to clean all enterprise data before starting. Pick one valuable AI use case, identify the data it depends on and bring that data up to standard. Each project improves the foundation for the next.

## Governance that enables, not blocks

Set clear policies for which data can be used with which AI tools, how outputs are reviewed and how models are monitored. Frameworks such as the NIST AI Risk Management Framework offer a structure. The goal is to let teams move quickly within safe boundaries, not to stop them.
