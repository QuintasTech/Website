Enterprise brands replatforming their commerce stack face the same first question: buy an integrated suite, or assemble best-of-breed services into a composable platform?

## What a suite gives you

A suite bundles storefront, catalogue, promotions, checkout and often order management under one vendor. You get a faster start, one contract and one support line. The trade-off is flexibility: when a market needs something the suite does not do well, you work around it.

## What composable gives you

A composable platform connects specialist services through APIs: commerce engine, content, search, payments and front end. Each piece can be replaced on its own schedule. The trade-off is ownership: someone has to design, integrate and run the whole system.

## Five questions to decide

1. **How many markets and brands** will run on the platform in three years?
2. **How different** are those markets in catalogue, pricing, tax and fulfilment?
3. **How strong** is your in-house engineering team, or your partner's?
4. **Which systems** must integrate on day one: ERP, PIM, OMS, CRM?
5. **What does total cost** look like over five years, including run costs?

## Our view

There is no universal answer. Brands with a few similar markets often move faster on a suite. Brands with many markets, frequent change and complex back offices usually get more value from composable, if they have a partner who can own integration and operations.
