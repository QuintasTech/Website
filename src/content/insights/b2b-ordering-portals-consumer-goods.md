Many consumer goods manufacturers still take a large share of distributor and retailer orders by email, phone, fax or spreadsheet. Customer service teams re-key them into the ERP, chase missing details and answer the same questions about stock and delivery dates every day. It works, but it is slow, error-prone and expensive to scale.

A B2B ordering portal lets trade customers place and track orders themselves, on terms specific to their account.

## What buyers expect now

Business buyers use consumer e-commerce in their personal lives and increasingly expect the same experience at work:

- Search and browse a catalog with accurate product data and images.
- See their own negotiated prices, promotions and minimum order quantities.
- Check real-time stock availability and expected delivery dates.
- Reorder quickly from past orders or saved lists.
- Track orders, download invoices and manage returns without calling anyone.

## What the business gains

- **Lower cost to serve.** Fewer manually keyed orders means customer service time shifts from data entry to account management.
- **Fewer errors.** Orders flow directly into the ERP with validated product codes, prices and ship-to addresses.
- **Faster ordering.** Customers can order at any hour, and orders arrive in a consistent format.
- **Better data.** Visibility into what customers browse and order helps sales and demand planning.

## The parts that matter most

**ERP integration.** The portal is only as good as its connection to your ERP for prices, inventory, orders and invoices. Decide early which system is the source of truth for each.

**Customer-specific pricing and catalogs.** Consumer goods pricing is often complex: contract prices, tiered discounts, promotional allowances, case and pallet quantities. The platform must handle these without heavy customization.

**Product information.** Clean, complete product data is often the biggest project in a portal launch. A product information management (PIM) tool may be worthwhile if data lives in many places.

**Account structures.** Distributors often have multiple buyers, approvers and ship-to locations. Support for roles and approval workflows matters.

## Choosing a platform

Options range from B2B features within platforms such as Shopify Plus, Adobe Commerce, BigCommerce and Salesforce Commerce Cloud, to ERP-native portals and composable builds. The right choice depends on your ERP, the complexity of pricing, whether you also sell direct to consumers and the size of your in-house team.

## Rolling it out

Start with a group of willing customers, often mid-sized accounts who order frequently. Make the portal the easiest way to order, not a mandate, and support customers through their first few orders. Track adoption, order accuracy and customer service time saved.
