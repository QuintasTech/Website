Outsourcing IT is often presented as all or nothing: either your team runs everything, or a provider takes it over. For most mid-sized and large enterprises, neither extreme fits. Co-managed IT sits in between. Your internal team keeps ownership and the work they do best, and a partner fills specific gaps.

## Why organizations choose it

- **After-hours coverage.** Running a true 24x7 operation in house needs enough staff for shifts, holidays and sickness. A partner can cover nights and weekends at a fraction of the cost.
- **Specialist skills.** Security operations, network engineering, cloud architecture and database administration are hard to hire for and may not be needed full time.
- **Capacity for projects.** When routine operations are shared, the internal team has time for the strategic work the business is asking for.
- **Resilience.** Less dependence on one or two individuals who hold critical knowledge.

## Common models

- **Monitoring and first response.** The partner watches systems around the clock, handles routine alerts and escalates to your team for anything significant.
- **Domain ownership.** The partner fully manages one area, such as the network, backup or security monitoring, while your team runs the rest.
- **Overflow and projects.** The partner provides engineers for peaks in demand or specific projects.
- **Service desk.** The partner handles user support while your team focuses on infrastructure and applications.

## Making it work

Co-managed arrangements succeed or fail on clarity. Agree:

- **A shared toolset**, or at least integration between your ticketing and monitoring tools and the partner's, so nothing falls between systems.
- **A responsibility matrix** showing who owns each task and who must be consulted.
- **Escalation paths** with named contacts on both sides.
- **Change control** that both parties follow.
- **Shared documentation** kept current by both teams.

Treat the partner's engineers as an extension of your team. Include them in planning meetings and post-incident reviews. The best co-managed relationships are ones where users cannot tell who fixed the problem.

## Questions to ask a provider

1. Will you work in our tools, or require us to use yours?
2. Who will our engineers deal with day to day, and how stable is that team?
3. How do you hand over between your staff and ours at shift boundaries?
4. How do you measure and report your performance?
