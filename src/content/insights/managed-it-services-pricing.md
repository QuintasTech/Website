Comparing managed services proposals is notoriously difficult. Providers use different pricing models, define scope differently and bundle services in different ways. Understanding what drives the cost makes it easier to compare like with like and to negotiate a fair agreement.

## Common pricing models

- **Per user.** A monthly fee for each user, typically covering service desk and endpoint management. Simple and predictable, best suited to user-focused services.
- **Per device or asset.** A fee per server, network device, endpoint or cloud resource. Common for infrastructure monitoring and management.
- **Tiered packages.** Bundles at different service levels, such as monitoring only, monitoring plus remediation, or fully managed.
- **Fixed fee for a defined scope.** A single monthly price for a documented estate and set of services, adjusted when the estate changes significantly.
- **Time and materials or block hours.** Pay for the work performed. Flexible but less predictable, often used for co-managed or overflow support.

Many agreements combine models, for example per-user service desk plus per-device infrastructure management.

## What moves the price

- **Coverage hours.** Round-the-clock support with fast response costs more than business-hours support.
- **Service levels.** Tighter response and resolution targets require more staff on standby.
- **Scope of responsibility.** Monitoring and alerting is cheaper than taking full responsibility for fixing problems.
- **Complexity.** Many sites, mixed platforms, legacy systems and custom applications all add effort.
- **Security services.** Security monitoring, managed detection and response, and vulnerability management are often priced separately.
- **On-site requirements.** Guaranteed on-site attendance at multiple locations adds cost.
- **Tooling and licensing.** Who pays for monitoring, backup, security and remote management tools.
- **Transition effort.** Onboarding, documentation and remediation of existing problems may be charged as a one-time project.

## How to compare proposals

1. **Give every bidder the same inventory and requirements**, including service levels and coverage hours.
2. **Ask for pricing broken down by service**, not just a total.
3. **Clarify what is excluded** and how out-of-scope work is charged.
4. **Check what happens when the estate grows or shrinks.**
5. **Compare total cost over the contract term**, including onboarding and any tooling you must buy.
6. **Weigh price against the cost of downtime.** The cheapest proposal can become the most expensive after one prolonged outage.

## Questions worth asking

- What is included in the base fee, and what triggers additional charges?
- How are price increases handled during the term?
- Are there minimum commitments or early termination fees?
